# ADHD psychology foundations for Grove

Short reference for product and engineering decisions. Citations point to peer-reviewed or widely cited clinical sources; Grove implications state what we should build.

## 1. Task initiation and paralysis

**Finding:** ADHD involves reduced prefrontal activation during goal-directed starts. Roughly two-thirds of adults report chronic difficulty beginning tasks even when they care about outcomes. Breaking work into the smallest visible step and body-doubling (working alongside someone) improve start rates more than motivational pep talks.

**Sources:** NIH/PMC reviews on executive dysfunction; Journal of Attention Disorders on initiation deficits.

**Grove implication:** Default flows must surface one tiny next action, not full project plans. Coach copy, Today tasks, and community sessions should emphasize “smallest step” and optional co-working, not guilt about unfinished lists.

## 2. Dopamine and the interest-based nervous system

**Finding:** Motivation in ADHD tracks interest, novelty, challenge, and urgency (NICE) more than importance alone. PET studies show differences in D2/D3 receptor availability in reward circuits (PMC 2958516).

**Sources:** PMC 2958516; clinical summaries of interest-based nervous system models.

**Grove implication:** XP and nudges should reward engagement when resistance is high, not only when tasks are “important.” Domain weights and coach suggestions should allow novelty and challenge as valid drivers.

## 3. Dopamine menu

**Finding:** Jessica McCabe and Eric Tivers popularized the “dopamine menu” (2020): short, categorized rewards (appetizers through desserts) chosen before depletion, with a reset ritual before returning to work.

**Sources:** How to ADHD community materials; ADHD Coaches Organisation practice guides.

**Grove implication:** Rewards and breaks should be pre-curated, sized, and easy to pick in under ten seconds. GRO-052 dopamine menu should use categories and a deliberate reset step, not an open-ended reward store.

## 4. Hyperfixation as motivational anchor

**Finding:** Voluntary hyperfocus can sustain effort; involuntary hyperfixation can drain recovery (“hangover”). Anchoring new goals to current fixations improves follow-through versus fighting the fixation.

**Sources:** Psychology Today and coaching literature on hyperfocus vs hyperfixation; qualitative ADHD accounts.

**Grove implication:** Track what the user is actually pursuing (top active goal by recent XP) and bonus tasks aligned with that anchor. Avoid shaming pivot energy; route planning through the current fixation when possible.

## 5. Timed focus sessions

**Finding:** Shorter blocks (often 10–15 minutes) outperform classic 25-minute pomodoros for many ADHD adults. Visible timers externalize time; breaks work best as earned rewards, not punishment.

**Sources:** JMIR and coaching studies on micro-sprints; occupational therapy timing recommendations.

**Grove implication:** Session timers and “find time” defaults should prefer short blocks with clear end signals. Break copy should celebrate completion, not imply failure for stopping.

## 6. Context switching and transition rituals

**Finding:** Switch costs are amplified with ADHD. Explicit closing rituals (note where you stopped) and opening rituals (one-line next action) reduce re-entry friction.

**Sources:** Cognitive psychology on task-switching; ADHD coaching protocols for transitions.

**Grove implication:** End-of-day journal and new-day debrief (GRO-042/043) are transition tools, not diaries for their own sake. Coach should reference last reflection when suggesting the first move.

## 7. Gamification evidence

**Finding:** Serious games meta-analyses report meaningful retention gains (~48% in some JMIR Serious Games 2022 syntheses). Variable rewards outperform fixed schedules early; fixed schedules risk hedonic adaptation if overused.

**Sources:** JMIR Serious Games 2022; behavioral economics on reward schedules.

**Grove implication:** XP should mix predictable progress (levels) with surprise unlocks, not inflate points every action. Community gates and granular levels should pace novelty without constant jackpots.

## 8. Planning and initiation XP

**Finding:** Starting is neurologically costlier than continuing once momentum exists. Interventions that reward initiation outperform those that only reward completion for habit formation.

**Sources:** ADHD executive function literature; behavioral activation research.

**Grove implication:** Add explicit planning bonus XP and difficulty bands so users are not forced to self-label effort. Hyperfixation bonus keeps the current anchor rewarding while initiation bonuses honor the hardest step.
